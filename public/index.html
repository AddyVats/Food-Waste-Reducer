How many portions should the kitchen cook?

Cooking exactly the average is a mistake: running out of a dish costs more than wasting one portion. This tool finds the batch size that minimises total cost, and shows what that saves over your current practice.

How the maths works

Let D be demand with mean μ and spread σ, and Q the portions cooked. Shortage cost per portion is cu and waste cost is co. The expected cost is minimised where the chance of covering demand equals the critical ratio:

F(Q*) = cu / (cu + co) so Q* = μ + z·σ, with z = Φ−1(critical ratio).

Expected leftovers are σ[zΦ(z) + φ(z)] and expected shortfall is σ[φ(z) − z(1 − Φ(z))], where φ and Φ are the standard normal density and cumulative distribution. Limits: demand is assumed roughly normal, and Q must be a whole number of portions, so the best batch is the better of the two whole numbers either side of the continuous optimum, and sales records hide demand on days when the dish sold out, so include those days with an estimate or treat the average as a lower bound.
